Where the money leaks at an apartment community

  • The owner's shareCommon areas, vacant units, leasing office, pools and the central plant stay on the owner's bills, even when utilities are billed back.
  • Resident billing that under-recoversA billing split that collects less from residents than the master bills cost, month after month.
  • Equipment running when it shouldn'tA booster pump running all night, a boiler short-cycling, a leaking fill valve. It shows on the bill before anyone sees it.
  • Sewer on water that never reaches the sewerPool, irrigation and cooling water billed for sewer. How it happens.
  • TurnoverMove-out and lease-up months, when power stays on in empty units and nobody is watching the bills.
  • Estimated reads and contract roll-offsMonths of estimates building to a catch-up bill; a supply contract quietly rolling onto a variable price.
Most of these aren't billing errors, so you keep 100% of the saving. We take a share only of refunds the utility actually pays back, never of savings.

Questions owners ask

We bill utilities back to residents. Do we still need this?

Usually, yes. The owner still pays for common areas, vacant units and central equipment, and a resident billing split can recover less than the master bills cost. We check both sides each month.

Do you replace our utility billing company?

No. Your billing company keeps paying the bills and billing residents. We check the master bills against the tariff and check what residents are billed against the master bills.
